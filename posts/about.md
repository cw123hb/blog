Hi, I'm the person behind Myriad Leaves.

I like languages the way some people like trainspotting — a bit too much,
and mostly for the small, specific details. Most of what ends up here comes
from noticing something odd: a line of dialogue that doesn't quite translate,
an honorific doing more work than it should, a word choice in an old
children's book that changes between editions. I write it up so I don't
forget it, and because I like the excuse to dig further.

A rough map of what you'll find, if you poke around the archive:

- **Japanese speech and grammar** — keigo, honorifics, pronouns, particles,
  and all the small mechanics that make characters *sound* like who they are.
- **Games and their scripts** — Street Fighter, Elden Ring, Dark Souls,
  Dragon Ball, and the odd, specific choices translators make when a fireball
  needs a name.
- **Translation comparisons** — the same text (a Dickens story, a fairy tale,
  a poem) set against different translations and different decades, to see
  what each one chose to keep.
- **Danish**, in smaller doses, because it's the other language I keep coming
  back to.
- **Christmas, Yule, and whatever seasonal nonsense** felt worth writing
  about that year.
- **Poems and short fiction** of my own, filed in with everything else.

There's no schedule and no house style beyond "write it while it's still
interesting." If you want to browse by topic rather than scroll the whole
archive, the categories on the [home page](#/) should help.

The blog runs on plain Markdown files with no build step — you're welcome to
look under the hood at the [source](https://codeberg.org/myriadleaves/blog).
